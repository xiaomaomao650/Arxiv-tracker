Corrupted but Correct: Why Vision-Language Models Lie to Themselves Internally
Authors: Arun Josephraj Arokiaraj, Chenyi Huang, Zekun Wu, Adriano Koshiyama
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-02T15:27:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:55:45+00:00
Comments: Accepted at the VLM4RWD Workshop (Grounded and Faithful Vision-Language Models for Real-World Deployment), NeurIPS 2026. 8 pages, 2 figures, 3 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A targeted adversarial perturbation can drive a vision-language model's (VLM's) teacher-forced training loss for a fixed target caption to near zero, yet the same model, allowed to generate freely, produces the original, correct description with no trace of the target. We call this dissociation the train/inference gap, and give it a precise mechanistic account on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct using a controlled two-stage PGD attack on 200 held-out COCO images. First, we show that image-level pixel statistics, including a correctly re-implemented, texture-based attackability measure from the CNN robustness literature, have essentially no predictive power over which images are corrupted (best predictor r=-0.050, p=0.484; ridge regression R^2=0.069). Second, using the logit lens, we localise the gap to a single autoregressive step: the rank of the target token, conditioned on the correct first token already being generated, is fixed at exactly 3,488 out of 152,064 vocabulary entries for every image and every condition, with zero variance. Third, tracking target-token rank across all 28 LLM decoder layers reveals that the visual encoder corrupts every image's representation by a comparable margin regardless of eventual outcome, but the language model decoder then differentially arbitrates: amplifying the corrupted signal for susceptible images and actively suppressing it, past its clean-image baseline, for resistant ones (p<0.001, rank-biserial r=0.579). A linear probe on the merger hidden state separates these two outcomes with AUC=0.858, though we flag a circularity concern in this estimate. Together these results argue that adversarial robustness in autoregressive VLMs is substantially a property of the language decoder's prior, not the visual encoder, with direct implications for where faithfulness evaluations and defenses for deployed VLM systems should be targeted.

Perceiving and Predicting Dynamic Sports: Spatiotemporal Intelligence in Vision Models for Egocentric Tennis Video
Authors: Tudor Braicu, Joseph DelPreto, Jared Boyer, Mónika Farsang, Maximilian Stölzle, Tim Niklas Seyde, Daniela Rus
First: 2026-10-27T17:47:14+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:47:14+00:00
Comments: 8 pages, 5 figures. Project page: https://tennis-vision.csail.mit.edu/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Agile robots require spatiotemporal intelligence to interpret scenes, reason about interactions, and anticipate future events. Yet it remains unclear how well foundation vision models support these capabilities, particularly from a moving egocentric camera, which is a natural view for robots. To investigate this, we collect an annotated tennis dataset and train a suite of vision models on spatiotemporal tasks. Our dataset contains egocentric video synchronized with five allocentric videos, estimated global positions, labeled contact times, and annotated stroke types. We then evaluate seven prediction tasks from egocentric images: 2D ego player and 3D ball localization, future player and ball trajectories, forehand or backhand stroke anticipation, time to the next ego player ball contact, and 3D ball position at that contact. We compare image encoders, video encoders, and vision-language models with jointly trained prediction heads. The best performing models achieve mean current-frame localization errors of 0.14 m for the ego player and 0.54 m for the ball, future forecasting errors of 0.34 m for the ego player and 0.91 m for the ball over 1.1 s, and a mean contact-time prediction error of 0.14 s. Auxiliary depth information substantially improves 3D ball localization, suggesting that inferring metric depth remains a challenge. Longer image histories do not improve ball forecasting, indicating that representations exploit scene context rather than rely solely on inter-frame motion. Finally, we propose a conditional diffusion model to generate whole-body kinematic tennis motion from vision encoder predictions on egocentric video at real-time rates, adapting movements as predictions are refined. These results move towards a deeper understanding of how foundation vision models can support real-time dynamic robots using egocentric video.

Which Hand Was Late? Diagnosis for Robot Failure Recovery in Bimanual Tool Use
Authors: Baptiste Bonin, Caro Strickland, Audrey Durand
First: 2026-10-27T16:53:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T16:53:18+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Failure diagnosers for robot manipulation are increasingly vision--language models (VLMs), trained and evaluated on single-arm failures produced by perturbing a successful demonstration. Bimanual tool use adds failures of other kinds: a tool that slips out of its socket while still held, two correct gestures made at different times, and attempts that fail for two reasons at once. On a simulated bimanual tool-use task whose failure causes are read from the recorded trace, we measure what each sensing channel lets a diagnoser tell. The tool's slip is read on the tool hand; which hand was late is read on both arms, while a sensor on one hand reads its own hand's delay and at most part of the other's; and whether a late attempt was also pushed too weakly is not identifiable from passive sensing when the tool hand is late. Within two attempts, a repair policy that escalates when the cause is not identifiable repairs as many failures as one told the true cause. A VLM's internal state carries the late hand almost perfectly, along a direction no closer to the one its answer reads than a random one; whether the answer reports it depends on the wording of the question.

Abstract
A robot policy needs to understand what a task is asking for in a scene, and how to move the robot to accomplish that task. To achieve these two skills, we introduce Zero-real Agent Policy (ZAP), a recipe that teaches a small off-the-shelf vision-language model to control a robot via a simple tool harness. We use a pretrained 27B parameter model to bootstrap world understanding and reasoning, followed by supervised fine-tuning and GRPO on simple simulated tasks to teach it how to move the robot. Without observing real-world robot data during training, ZAP reaches 96.7% success on real-world tasks, matching GPT-6 Astra in our harness and outperforming the MolmoAct2 VLA (33.3%), while running 3x faster and 17x cheaper than GPT-6 Astra. Furthermore, ZAP also generalizes to an unseen embodiment as well as new objects, goals and strategies never seen during training. On a single-arm Franka, ZAP reaches 55.9% on RoboLab-120, 4.9 percentage points ahead of the leading VLA/WAM baseline.

DRIFT: A Residual Flow Adapter for Decoding Continuous Outputs in Vision-Language Models
Authors: Zhuoming Liu, Jinhong Lin, Kwan Man Cheng, Lin Zhang, Shayok Bagchi, Yin Li
First: 2026-06-04T06:37:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T15:50:15+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Many modern vision-language models (VLMs) build on autoregressive decoding of discrete tokens. While text-based output interfaces enable scalable pretraining and strong zero-shot generalization across diverse tasks, they are poorly suited for problems that require precise continuous outputs, such as localizing temporal boundaries of events or generating robotic control actions. To address this challenge, we propose DRIFT, a general framework for adapting pretrained VLMs to continuous decoding tasks. DRIFT combines a base predictor, which provides a coarse estimate of the target output, with a generative refinement module based on flow matching that iteratively improves the prediction. This residual formulation transforms the generative modeling problem from learning a global output distribution to modeling a localized residual distribution around a strong prior, substantially simplifying optimization. We evaluate DRIFT on both perception and planning tasks, including visual grounding and robotic control. Across multiple tasks and architectures spanning MLLMs, VLAs, and WAMs, DRIFT consistently outperforms a strong set of regression- and generative-based solutions.

CTBakeGS: training-free baking of photorealistic CT volume renderings into 3D Gaussian splats
Authors: Haruki Fukuda, Constantin Lux, Wolf Schweitzer, Tadashi Hosoya, Lars Ebert
First: 2026-10-27T15:21:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T15:21:01+00:00
Comments: Code and project page: https://github.com/BocchiSlice/CTBakeGS
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Photorealistic volume rendering of computed tomography (CT) may convey anatomy intuitively, but it needs the CT data, dedicated software and a powerful graphics processor. Three-dimensional Gaussian splatting (3DGS) displays photorealistic scenes in real time on less powerful devices, but existing medical approaches train each scene for minutes to hours. We aimed to convert a CT volume rendering directly into a 3DGS model without training. Splats were placed on an adaptive octree of millimetre-sized cells. Their colour and opacity were computed on the graphics processor with the renderer's lighting, cell opacity was converted analytically into splat opacity, and faint splats were retained stochastically above the 1/255 opacity floor of standard 3DGS rasterisers. Six public CT scans (1.5-mm voxels) were converted with three transfer functions and compared with converged references by peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), structural similarity and a learned perceptual metric. Two-million-splat models reached a PSNR of 32.6 +/- 0.9 dB for bone, 30.1 +/- 1.0 dB for contrast-enhanced vessels and 32.3 +/- 1.5 dB for skin and bone. A conversion pass (usually two or three per model) took 4-30 s on a current laptop and 9-60 s on a desktop with a 4-GB graphics card. On the laptop, the models rendered at 68 frames per second at 800 x 800 pixels. Training-free conversion produced real-time 3DGS models of photorealistic CT renderings in about a minute or less per conversion pass on ordinary computers, with fidelity that we consider sufficient for interactive viewing.

TimeLineXR: An Interactive Exploration of Time in Vision-Language Models
Authors: Nidham Tekaya, Markus Passecker, Matthias Zeppelzauer

Abstract
Recently, it has been shown that vision-language models (VLMs) have a certain intrinsic understanding of time. They can estimate the temporal origin of objects and encode time along a low-dimensional, non-linear manifold inside their embedding space~\cite{tekaya2025matter}. That result is quantitative and reproducible, but the structure itself stays out of reach for end users: they cannot see it, query it with their own images, or inspect where a query lands relative to other years on the time axis. We present TimeLineXR, an interactive demonstration that turns this time axis into an explorable 3D scene. A web demo lets users upload or select images and see each placed on a color-coded 3D time axis; a Three.js WebXR scene renders the same time axis as a room-scale representation that can be grabbed, scaled, inspected, and queried by throwing image cards into it. Both clients run the same prediction pipeline against the same cached temporal index: encode the query with CLIP, project it through cosine-kernel KPCA, and snap it to the Bézier time axis. The system turns each image into a point on the time axis, so users can compare where different objects land and read off their estimated first year of appearance.Code source and reproduction steps can be found in: https://github.com/sterngefeuert/timeline-xr.

SQuID: A Benchmark for Quantitative Spatial Reasoning on Satellite Imagery

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) describe satellite images fluently but struggle with the quantitative spatial reasoning that remote sensing (RS) analysts perform routinely, such as counting buildings, measuring forest area in hectares and computing distances in meters. We introduce SQuID (Satellite Quantitative Intelligence Dataset), a benchmark of 2,000 questions over 587 images from four sources at 0.3 to 1.0 m ground sampling distance, covering 24 question types in three difficulty tiers. Answers are computed from curated segmentation masks or, for 50 questions, from human consensus, and numeric answers are scored against tolerance ranges informed by human annotations. SQuID also releases the reference masks behind its answers, so that modular systems can be evaluated using reference rather than predicted masks, and 75,687 training question-answer pairs on disjoint images (SQuID-Train). Among 15 evaluated systems, the best zero-shot end-to-end VLM reaches 28.1%, and RS-specialized VLMs score lower. As a baseline, we propose QASR (Quantitative Agentic Spatial Reasoning), in which an LLM writes a program that segments the image and measures the masks with geometric operators. QASR reaches 42.0%. On the 1,950 mask-derived questions, running its programs on the reference masks instead of predicted masks raises accuracy from 42.2% to 82.6%, so segmentation is its main source of error. Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on SQuID-Train raises its accuracy from 16.1% to 40.7%, close to QASR. Multi-condition questions (Tier 3) remain the hardest in every setting, at 62.0% for QASR with reference masks and 13.2% after fine-tuning. These results leave clear room for end-to-end models that learn metric spatial reasoning, and SQuID provides the data to train and evaluate them.

Hierarchical Sparse Graph for Multi-dimensional Assignment in Event-based Tracking
Authors: Muxi Zha, Yang Shang, Wenlong Zhang, Qifeng Yu, Banglei Guan
First: 2026-10-27T14:34:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T14:34:07+00:00
Comments: 16 pages, published to IEEE T-CSVT
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Existing event-based tracking methods typically operate in an online manner, associating tracklets across adjacent batches. However, this limited temporal window often leads to failures in challenging scenarios, such as long-term occlusions or missed detections, where broader temporal context should be incorporated for high-precision tracking. To achieve robust and accurate tracking, this paper concentrates on the event-based multi-dimensional assignment that associates tracklets across multiple batches simultaneously. Unlike image-based detectors, event-based detectors can generate multiple correct yet spatially distinct tracklets for a single object per batch. This violates the one-to-one rule in classic data association, making event-based association an underdetermined many-to-many matching problem. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to tackle this NP-hard problem. To address its underdetermined nature, we formulate the assignment problem via fuzzy logic, where pairwise tracklet correlations are quantified through multiple long-range kinematic metrics. This fuzzy design eliminates the need for hard tracking thresholds and enables multiple heterogeneous metrics to be integrated into a unified correlation. By treating tracklets as nodes and their fuzzy correlations as edges, we construct a Hierarchical Sparse Graph (HSG). Building upon the HSG, we pose assignment as a graph partition task, and employ a heuristic solution that measures vertex affinity for partitioning adaptively. The entire pipeline is training-free. Comprehensive experiments on two challenging event datasets, eTram and Ev-UAV, show that our method outperforms existing event-based and most image-based association models. Particularly, it achieves over 2$\times$ the performance of OC-SORT across all metrics in the many-to-many association scenario.

An Embarrassingly Simple Data Selection Strategy for Million-Scale Multi-Modal Instruction-Tuning

Abstract
Recent research demonstrates that pretrained large language models (LLMs) require minimal but carefully curated data for instruction-tuning. Building on LLMs, multi-modal models further contend with far larger data volumes, heterogeneous sources, and cross-modal alignment, amplifying both the necessity and the complexity of data selection. However, existing methods rely on deeply extracted embeddings, gradients or narrow quality scores that are time-consuming to compute and limited in expressiveness, frequently failing to surpass a simple random-sampling baseline. To overcome these limitations, we step back from complex embedding spaces and target the fundamental objective of instruction tuning: how and what knowledge should be elicited from the pretrained model and exposed to users. We leverage interaction \underline{S}tyles as a proxy for structural diversity, and decompose data quality into capability-specific \underline{S}cores, yielding an explicit yet \underline{R}ich (SSR) profile for each sample. We further cast the selection as submodular coverage maximization over the capability--style grid, providing an approximation guarantee. SSR consistently delivers superior performance across two distinct architecture stacks, LLaVA-1.5 (Vicuna + CLIP) and LLaVA-OneVision (Qwen2 + SigLIP), two large-scale data pools, six data budget settings, and 14 multi-modal benchmarks. To our knowledge, SSR is the first data selection method validated at million-scale, achieving 99.1% of full-data performance using only 30% of a 2.6M pool. Furthermore, the SSR profile models and selected subsets transfer training-free across data pools and model architectures with capability extensibility.

MvT: Event-based Multi-view Projection for Multiple Object Tracking
Authors: Muxi Zha, Banglei Guan, Minzu Liang, Zibin Liu, Yang Shang, Qifeng Yu, Laurent Kneip
First: 2026-10-27T14:26:53+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T14:26:53+00:00
Comments: 16 pages, 20 figures, published to IEEE T-IP
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Event cameras are increasingly used for Multiple Object Tracking (MOT), but their asynchronous event output often requires specialized methods. Existing processing methods primarily follow two paradigms, pseudo-frames and event-by-event. The former is the prevailing approach since its data format aligns with images, making image-based techniques applicable. However, it suffers from tracking failures when trajectories overlap or are spatially close on pseudo-frames. Facing this challenge, we propose a multi-view pipeline, Multi-view Tracking (MvT), which preserves the 2D data format to leverage image-based techniques directly while introducing additional spatio-temporal views to resolve tracking ambiguities in a single view. MvT comprises a Multi-view Projection (MvP) module and a Multi-view Fusion (MvF) stage. MvP encodes events into three complementary spatio-temporal views while mitigating the pattern discretization. Within MvF, multi-view results are unified into a 3D coordinate system, and tracklets are associated through an optimization model subject to specific criteria combination. Evaluations on four datasets, including our self-collected Small Objects Dataset (SOD), show that MvT seamlessly integrates image-based methods and outperforms existing non-learning and learning trackers in generalized scenarios, and effectively resolves the single-view tracking ambiguities. Being training-free, MvT is applicable when ground-truth annotation is infeasible, thereby highlighting its practical, data-efficient potential. Code is available at https://github.com/zhazhabiu/MvTracking.

Combinatorial Lock for Trojan-Resilient AI Engines via Hash-Assisted Spectral Filtering
Authors: Soumita Chatterjee, Sudip Ghosh, Tamal Ghosh, Bhargab B. Bhattacharya, Hafizur Rahaman
First: 2026-10-24T04:26:13+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T14:09:43+00:00

Abstract
Multi-trigger backdoor attacks, in which heterogeneous trigger mechanisms (e.g., patch, blend, warping, and the latent-domain Spy-Watermark) coexist within a single poisoned model, cannot be addressed by existing shortcut-based defenses that assume a single, static attack mechanism. Current multi-trigger remedies (BU+FT, BU+ANP, and BU+ABL) rely on backdoor unlearning, which incurs high training costs and loses effectiveness at low poisoning ratios. This paper proposes Combinatorial Lock, a training-free, reference-calibrated defense that reduces multi-trigger identification to a combinatorial problem over binary activation signatures. This formulation is the key novelty: it yields a multi-trigger defense that is training-free, trigger-oblivious, and directly implementable in hardware, since its decision rules can be evaluated with integer and logic operations. The framework combines anomaly-gated spectral scrubbing, which removes the malicious subspace only from inputs identified as anomalous, with a hash-compressed combinatorial signature verified by an ensemble of detection experts, including a compound-attack-aware expert for multi-trigger inputs. A joint threshold-shrinkage search identifies an operating point ($τ$ at the 97.1st percentile, $γ= 0.78$) that reduces the attack success rate (ASR) to as low as 0.1% while preserving 97.8% clean accuracy. Evaluation on six models (ResNet18, VGG16, MobileNet, AlexNet, ViT-Small, ViT-Base) shows strong generalization: near-100% ASR drops to single digits across poisoning rates of 1-10% and 1-9 simultaneous triggers, with only gradual degradation in clean accuracy. ASIC and FPGA implementations confirm practicality, adding a consistent 19-20% latency overhead across all evaluated architectures and establishing Combinatorial Lock as a hardware-native approach to multi-trigger backdoor mitigation.

Abstract
With the proliferation of AI-generated images, digital watermarking has become an essential safeguard for protecting intellectual property and mitigating malicious exploitation. Recent works on semantic watermarking have enabled efficient copyright protection for diffusion models. However, the dependence of semantic watermarking on diffusion inversion for watermark detection creates a critical vulnerability. Imprint removal and forgery attacks exploit this weakness to produce deceptive results. Our analysis reveals that these attacks succeed by displacing watermarked latents into the unwatermarked region, while guiding unwatermarked latents into the watermarked region. Based on that, we propose Progressive Guided Inversion and Denoising (PGID), the first plug-and-play, training-free noise extraction framework designed to defend against both attack strategies. PGID effectively defends by projecting perturbed latents back to the region where they originally belong. The projection is achieved by eliminating intermediate latent deflections and mitigating adversarial perturbations through progressive inversion-denoising cycles. Comprehensive evaluations across multiple schemes demonstrate that PGID successfully restores detection reliability by recovering removed watermarks and identifying forged instances.

Vision-Language-Action or World-Action? Just Action Models
Authors: Yonghao Yu, Lang Huang, Runyi Li, Toshihiko Yamasaki
First: 2026-10-27T13:29:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T13:29:28+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
The vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action model (WAM) communities debate whether control policies should inherit pretrained knowledge from vision-language models or from visual generators. The debate, however, bundles three separable choices: backbone initialization, the objective applied to future observations, and whether the machinery for that future loss is kept at inference. A gap between a VLA and a WAM therefore cannot simply be attributed to any one of them. We show that decoupling these choices yields a common framework for both families, which we term Just Action Model (JAM): a pretrained visual backbone with an action expert, trained with an action loss and a foresight loss. The foresight loss trains current-observation features to predict frozen encodings of the recorded future through a prediction head discarded after training, so no visual generation is involved in either training or inference, and the backbone can be chosen freely. We first compare objectives within fixed policy architectures, then evaluate the foresight recipe across backbone families. The resulting JAM policies outperform their matched FastWAM and ImageWAM counterparts on LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus, and achieve similar or slightly higher success rates on RoboTwin. Compared with FastWAM, JAM improves the success rates from 97.0%/55.3%/91.2% to 98.2%/64.5%/91.4%, respectively. With cached foresight targets, JAM reduces estimated online training FLOPs per sample per update by 73% relative to FastWAM. Our results show that future supervision improves control without visual generation or additional inference machinery, and strong foresight performance is attainable with either generator-initialized or vision-language backbones. GCSR-OCC: Geometry Calibration and Semantic Routing for Open-Vocabulary Occupancy Prediction
Authors: Bohua Wang, Congyang Zhao, Tianxiong Lv, Chenxu Zhao, He Zheng, Hangqi Fan, Leigang Luo, Zhihao Liao, Yihong Guo, Yang Cai
First: 2026-10-27T12:47:53+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T12:47:53+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Open-vocabulary 3D occupancy prediction represents scene geometry and semantics in a unified voxel grid, enabling recognition beyond predefined categories. One approach reconstructs scenes with 3D Gaussians and converts them into voxel occupancy. However, Gaussian scale inflation and excessive overlap can distort voxel occupancy predictions, while semantic mixing can arise within Gaussians spanning multiple categories and during 3D--2D semantic feature alignment. We propose GCSR-OCC, which combines geometry calibration and multi-mode query-conditioned semantic routing for indoor occupancy prediction from a single RGB image. The Evidence-Conserving Gaussian-to-Occupancy operator (EC-G2O) uses kernel mass normalization, $p$-norm aggregation, and a learnable gain to suppress scale inflation and excessive local evidence accumulation while calibrating occupancy responses. Multi-mode Query-First Gaussian Semantic Routing (MM-QGSR) matches multiple semantic modes per Gaussian to text queries and aggregates query-specific evidence into voxels through a learnable text-conditioned router. Fine-grained subclass queries and smoothed semantic aggregation further refine voxel-level predictions. Without 3D semantic annotations, GCSR-OCC achieves state-of-the-art performance in open-vocabulary occupancy prediction on Occ-ScanNet, with 63.31% geometric IoU and 27.44% semantic mIoU. It also reduces geometric aggregation latency from 19.76 ms to 12.88 ms. Our code will be made publicly available.

CLEAR: Coupling Local Evidence with Affective Representations for Multimodal Emotion Recognition in Conversation
Authors: Linan Zhu, Zihao Zhai, Mengfei Xu, Xiao Han, Yuqian Fu, Xiangfan Chen, Zihang Zhang
First: 2026-05-18T15:27:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T12:22:55+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In video calls and livestreamed discussions, recognizing speakers' emotions helps identify emotionally salient segments and supports fine-grained video content analysis. Multimodal Emotion Recognition in Conversation (ERC) provides an approach to recognizing emotions in such content by integrating textual, visual, and acoustic information with conversational context. Current mainstream emotion recognition methods largely leverage the contextual understanding capabilities of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to predict emotions by generating labels. However, emotion understanding and label prediction may be inconsistent, leaving the internal affective information underutilized. Moreover, when the current utterance is jointly modeled with extensive dialogue history and multimodal information, its local discriminative cues may be diluted and become less salient in the resulting contextual representation. To address these limitations, we propose CLEAR, an ERC framework that couples multimodal contextual representations with local discriminative cues. To mitigate the mismatch between emotion understanding and label prediction, we use the continuous internal representation of an emotion-supervised vision-language model (VLM) instead of its generated emotion labels, and further train a classifier based on this representation. To strengthen local discriminative cues, we use sample-adaptive residual coupling to dynamically integrate textual and acoustic evidence from the target utterance into the contextual representation. Experiments on IEMOCAP and MELD demonstrate that CLEAR achieves competitive performance, supporting the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The code is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/vlm-210F/.

Abstract

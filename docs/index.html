SUREON: A Benchmark and Vision-Language Model for Surgical Reasoning
Authors: Alejandra Perez, Anita Rau, Busisiwe Mlambo, Lee White, Aina Brunet, Juan Verde, Juan Caicedo, Joël L. Lavanchy, Adam Schmidt, Chinedu Nwoye, Omid Mohareri, Muhammad Abdullah Jamal
Venue: WACV 2027
First: 2026-03-06T18:58:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T17:58:13+00:00
Comments: WACV 2027
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A capable surgical AI model should reason from visual evidence; yet, many existing surgical benchmarks can be partially solved without visual input, as models may exploit procedural priors or common anatomy-instrument interactions to infer answers from language alone. To address this, we introduce SUREON, a video question-answering (QA) dataset and benchmark designed to evaluate visually grounded surgical reasoning. Our pipeline generates QA pairs from narrated surgical academic videos, refines incorrect options using multiple models to reduce linguistic biases, and uses trained adversarial guessers to filter out questions answerable without visual evidence. The resulting dataset contains 76K training pairs covering 168 procedures and 10 specialties, alongside a benchmark of 340 questions across 10 categories verified by expert surgeons. Benchmarking leading commercial and open-source models shows that surgical visual reasoning remains largely unsolved--no model exceeds 53% accuracy and even expert surgeons reach only 64.9%. We also introduce SUREON-VLM, a baseline trained on our curated data that avoids language shortcuts, outperforms existing open-source models on SUREON, and matches larger specialist models on multiple standard surgical benchmarks. Our training ablations show that distractor refinement and blind curation strengthen visual grounding, increasing the multimodal gain from 8.7 to 29.1 percentage points while reducing the training set from 176.6K to 76K filtered samples. SUREON thus offers both a verified testbed for surgical visual reasoning and training data that strengthens visual grounding.

Windowed-MTP: Removing the Full-Context Draft-KV Tax at Million-Token Context
Authors: Alagappan Valliappan
First: 2026-07-23T17:21:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T17:48:26+00:00
Comments: 29 pages, 5 figures, 16 tables. v2: Windowed draft attention is merged upstream in SGLang (PR #32673). Code: https://github.com/avalliappan-nvidia/windowed-mtp-b200
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Speculative decoding accelerates autoregressive generation by having a draft propose tokens that a target verifies in parallel. Frontier models increasingly ship a built-in Multi-Token-Prediction (MTP) draft head, assumed to be cheap beside verification. A shipped full-attention MTP head can become disproportionately expensive beside a hybrid target at long context. The head attends over the full KV cache for each of $γ$ draft forwards per decode step, while only 9-25% of target layers retain full attention, so a single draft layer can issue context reads at a rate comparable to the whole target. On hard, low-acceptance workloads, deep native MTP becomes net-negative: slower than no speculation. We apply a StreamingLLM-style window plus attention sink to the draft only (Windowed-MTP); target verification remains full-context, so the target distribution is preserved. The intervention is training-free and drop-in. On three models from two hybrid families (Gated DeltaNet and Mamba2) at 1M context on one GPU in SGLang, it provides an acceptance-free 1.28-1.44x per-decode-step speedup over native MTP. The speedup grows with context; at matched acceptance, TPOT improves by the same factor. The window removes most of the draft's working set, and a compact ring buffer converts that reduction into physical capacity: it reclaims the otherwise dead draft pool, a measured 7.7-11.1% of total KV at 1M, making room for one additional concurrent 1M request in the Qwen-35B batch sweep.

VisualSphinx: Large-Scale Synthetic Vision Logic Puzzles for RL

Abstract
Vision language models (VLMs) are expected to perform effective multimodal reasoning and make logically coherent decisions, which is critical to tasks such as diagram understanding and spatial problem solving. However, current VLM reasoning lacks large-scale and well-structured training datasets. To bridge this gap, we propose VisualSphinx, a first-of-its-kind large-scale synthetic visual logical reasoning training dataset. To tackle the challenge of image synthesis with grounded answers, we propose a rule-to-image synthesis pipeline that extracts and expands puzzle rules from seed questions and generates executable rendering programs for puzzle assembly. Using standard GRPO with binary correctness rewards as a validation recipe, experiments show that VLMs trained on VisualSphinx benefit from the logical coherence and readability of our dataset and exhibit improved performance on logical reasoning tasks. The enhanced reasoning capabilities developed from VisualSphinx also benefit other reasoning tasks such as algebraic reasoning, arithmetic reasoning and geometry reasoning.

Any Forcing: Any-Step Generality and One-Step Stability for Autoregressive Video Generation
Authors: Jiahua Xu, Yijie Fang, Nannan Wang, Xunhao Lin, Li Cao, Lei Hao, Mingrui Zhu, Fei Gao, Xinbo Gao
First: 2026-10-30T16:56:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T16:56:42+00:00

Abstract
Autoregressive video generation enables efficient inference through streaming prediction. However, existing methods are typically restricted to fixed sampling steps, limiting the flexibility to balance generation quality and efficiency, while one-step generation suffers from degraded visual quality and error accumulation over long sequences, leading to content drift and generation collapse. To address these issues, we propose Any Forcing, a unified framework for achieving Any-Step Generality and One-Step Stability. Specifically, we introduce a unified adversarial discriminator that jointly performs real-distribution alignment, inter-step consistency alignment, and first-frame quality enhancement, enabling flexible sampling while preserving one-step generation quality. Furthermore, we propose a training-free adaptive long-term memory selection mechanism that selects historical states based on global semantic relevance and local value contribution, while remapping their temporal positions to preserve temporal relationships within the memory cache, thereby enabling stable one-step long-video generation. Experiments demonstrate that Any Forcing achieves high-quality and stable one-step autoregressive video generation, achieving 20.9 FPS and 24.7 FPS for short and long video generation on a single H100 GPU, respectively.

Abstract
Vector Quantization (VQ) is a rapidly evolving technique for compressing Large Language Model (LLM) weights, reducing memory usage, and speeding up inference. However, extreme compression deteriorates model performance, which is challenging to recover due to the discrete nature of VQ parameters. Existing methods employ training-free approaches that are limited to layer-wise reconstruction loss minimization. To address this limitation, we propose TVQ, a novel method that leverages straight-through estimation to enable gradient descent-based optimization of vector-quantized LLMs. By reformulating VQ LLM optimization with trainable latent weights, TVQ enables arbitrary objective minimization. We constitute our approach by applying TVQ in a block-wise knowledge distillation paradigm, which balances quality and training efficiency, and propose several practical solutions to accelerate training. In experiments on modern LLMs, we demonstrate that TVQ is compatible with modern VQ schemes. As a result, TVQ achieves state-of-the-art quality in low-bit post-training quantization settings with a modest training budget and straightforward PyTorch implementation. For instance, TVQ achieves near-lossless 2-bit quantization for LLaMA2-70B with only 1% accuracy drop on popular Question-Answering benchmarks.

Abstract
Neural Architecture Search (NAS) has advanced the automated design of deep networks, but remains computationally expensive due to reliance on model training or performance predictors. We introduce DIFFNAS, a predictor-free NAS framework that leverages a discrete graph diffusion model guided by zero-cost performance proxies. Unlike prior diffusion-based NAS methods that require learned performance predictors, DIFFNAS conditions the generative process on training-free metrics such as MeCo and SWAP, enabling efficient architecture search with minimal computational overhead. Experiments on NAS-Bench-201 show that DIFFNAS finds the optimal architectures for CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 (94.37% and 73.51%) without a pretrained predictor, while reducing search cost by up to 17.5x compared to DiffusionNAG. On the MobileNetV3 search space, its accuracy is lower than that of DiffusionNAG. These results highlight the promise of diffusion models combined with zero-cost proxies for scalable and effective architecture search.

Latent-Lens: Visual Perception in Small Language Models Through Latent Communication
Authors: Mo Shahdloo, Faegheh Sardari, Mathew Salvaris
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-30T13:53:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T13:53:44+00:00
Comments: Published at NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Bridging independent vision-language models (VLMs) and small language models (SLMs) in a modular multi-agent system typically forces them to exchange information as discrete text, which creates a lossy bottleneck for spatial and fine-grained details. This constraint is especially acute for SLMs, whose limited capacity makes them more reliant on the quality of the input signal. Recent latent-communication methods exchange states between language models. How a VLM can pass visual information to a separately pretrained text-only SLM though remains unexplored. We propose \textbf{Latent-Lens}, a lightweight codec that bridges the representation gap between a frozen VLM and a frozen SLM directly in latent space, enabling them to communicate purely through hidden states. The codec consists of a domain encoder that projects hidden states from the VLM into the SLM's embedding space, and an attention decoder formalised as a LoRA that teaches the SLM to attend to these visual prefixes, while training only ${\sim}1.6$M parameters (0.2\% of the combined model size). On visual question answering (CLEVR, GQA), Latent-Lens achieves $81.0\pm7.9$\% and $65.4\pm5.4$\%, compared with $58.3\pm5.7$\% and $61.8\pm1.0$\% for text communication with the same LoRA budget (+30.2--69.8pp relative to frozen text communication), while on image captioning (Flickr8k) it improves over the same baseline by +16.5 (+18.8 frozen). We also evaluate Latent-Lens with TinyLlama-1.1B and InternVL2-1B, which use different architectures, vocabularies, and hidden dimensions.

GROME: GROunding Factorized MEmory into Video World Models
Authors: Fang Yu, Xiaoxue Wu, Haiyu Zhang, Yangfu Li, Botong Zhao, Yu Qiao, Tianfan Xue, Yue Lu, Xinyuan Chen
First: 2026-10-29T10:54:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T13:16:34+00:00

Abstract
Long-horizon consistency is essential for video world models, yet it becomes difficult to maintain once observed content falls outside the context window. Existing memory mechanisms either retrieve history as implicit context, which remains constrained by context capacity and entangles static and dynamic information or consolidate history into explicit global representations, which struggle to model evolving dynamics and accumulate geometric errors. To overcome these limitations, we introduce GROME, a novel factorized memory framework that augments video world models for long-horizon coherence across both static scenes and dynamic entities. GROME operates via three synergistic components: 1) Static-Dynamic Factorization decouples historical evidence into static and dynamic components, preserving static scene geometry and dynamic entity states separately; 2) Local Memory Retrieval selects spatially aligned static history to eliminate geometric drift alongside the latest dynamic entity states to ensure temporal continuity; 3) Training-Free Memory Injection seamlessly grounds the factorized memory into video world models via their native conditioning pathways. Experiments on long-horizon revisit scenarios demonstrate that GROME substantially enhances scene persistence and dynamic entity fidelity over existing methods, all while preserving the visual quality and controllability of the frozen backbone without additional training. Beyond self-rollouts, GROME readily accommodates external observations as plug-and-play memory, unlocking flexible and controllable video continuation and scene exploration.

Open-Vocabulary Models and Label Propagation for Uncertainty-aware Pseudo-Labeling of Object Detection Datasets
Authors: Jonathan Klees, Timo Heller, Matthias Rottmann
First: 2026-10-21T14:15:35+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T12:50:34+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Object detection models rely on large-scale annotated datasets that require substantial manual annotation effort. The labeling costs further increase when annotation uncertainty is captured through soft labels, as multiple annotations must be collected for each object. We address this challenge by introducing a semi-supervised framework for the uncertainty-aware annotation of object detection datasets. Our approach leverages open-vocabulary object detectors to generate region proposals and semantically meaningful object representations. Based on these representations, we construct an object-level similarity graph and propagate probabilistic label information. Our framework assigns soft labels to previously unlabeled objects, quantifying both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty. We evaluate our method on four object detection benchmarks with soft-label annotations and demonstrate that high-quality annotations can be obtained with only a small fraction of manually labeled objects. Furthermore, we show that detectors trained on semi-automatically annotated data achieve performance comparable to models trained on fully manually annotated data. We achieve state-of-the-art downstream detection performance for the task of semi-supervised object detection on the COCO dataset, with 40.5% AP virtually matching the performance of the model trained on fully manually constructed data (40.9%) at only 1% of labeling costs. These results highlight the potential of open-vocabulary models and graph-based label propagation for efficient, accurate and uncertainty-aware pseudo-labeling.

Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) have made significant progress in spatial reasoning, particularly by integrating RGB images with depth data, which is often assumed to faithfully reflect physical distances. However, mirrors violate this assumption: they distort depth readings while reproducing object appearances, making reflections difficult to distinguish from real objects. As existing benchmarks rarely consider such reflective scenarios, VLM performance in mirror scenes remains largely unexplored. In this work, we introduce a mirror-centric RGB-D dataset from reflective scenes to evaluate the spatial reasoning capabilities of state-of-the-art VLMs in these scenarios. Specifically, we collect 1,011 RGB-D images from 525 real-world scenes, spanning diverse indoor and outdoor environments, six reflective surface types, and both daytime and nighttime conditions. All images are annotated with reflective surface region masks and mask annotations for both directly observed and mirror-reflected objects. Then, we curate a benchmark named MIRA-Bench (Mirror-Induced Reasoning Ambiguity Benchmark), comprising 14,140 QA pairs across eight sub-tasks organized into three cognitive levels: Reflection-aware Perception, Spatial Reasoning, and Scene-level Decision-making. We evaluate state-of-the-art VLMs on MIRA-Bench and find consistent failures across tasks: models misclassify whether objects are directly observed or reflected, fail to match reflected instances to their real-world counterparts, and produce erroneous spatial inferences. Remarkably, providing depth data actually degrades reasoning performance, implying that VLMs cannot identify incorrect depth in mirror scenes. The code and data are available at https://github.com/EvaYin-cloud/MIRA-Bench

GRIT: Gradient Masking for Visually Misgrounded Tokens in Text-Rich Reasoning
Authors: Chanseul Cho, Noori Bae, Dongheon Lee, Youngmin Ro

Abstract
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has recently been extended to vision-language models (VLMs), yet its effectiveness remains limited for text-rich reasoning tasks. We highlight a key factor contributing to this limitation: models frequently arrive at correct final answers despite misperceiving visual information during intermediate steps. We define this phenomenon as Correct-but-Visually-Misgrounded Reasoning (CVMR). Because standard RLVR rewards only final-answer correctness, it fails to differentiate between properly grounded and misgrounded reasoning, reinforcing both equally. Through token-level analysis, we reveal that CVMR tokens exhibit distinct attention patterns that differ systematically from well-grounded ones. Based on this observation, we propose GRIT, a training framework that incorporates visual grounding into RLVR to mitigate the impact of CVMR. Furthermore, to address the lack of text-rich reasoning benchmarks that allow for systematic evaluation across text length and reasoning depth, we introduce GRIT-Bench. Experiments demonstrate that GRIT significantly improves both reasoning accuracy and grounding fidelity over existing RLVR methods.

Attention Maps Know When to Think: Training-Free Difficulty-Aware Chain-of-Thought for Reasoning Segmentation
Authors: Chanseul Cho, Jaesung Jun, Dongheon Lee, Youngmin Ro
Venue: WACV 2027
First: 2026-10-30T11:56:54+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T11:56:54+00:00

Abstract
Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting has become a standard reasoning strategy in Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs), showing strong performance in complex vision-language tasks such as reasoning segmentation. However, our analysis reveals that indiscriminate CoT usage can lead to degraded performance by inducing unnecessary reasoning, a phenomenon we term overthinking. In reasoning segmentation, overthinking often leads to misalignment between the query's intent and visual grounding. To address this, we propose Difficulty-Aware Chain-of-Thought (DA-CoT), a training-free adaptive framework that selectively triggers CoT based on the input's intrinsic difficulty. We find that the entropy of the model's attention maps reliably reflects task difficulty: low entropy indicates confident, localized grounding, while high entropy suggests uncertainty and potential benefit from CoT. DA-CoT uses this entropy signal to determine whether to apply CoT prompting. Additionally, we introduce a Bidirectional Rethinking mechanism that enables the model to automatically flip its reasoning decision when the initial output fails, enhancing robustness by recovering from failures induced by overthinking and underthinking. DA-CoT demonstrates its effectiveness by achieving state-of-the-art performance on the Reasoning Segmentation benchmarks while significantly improving inference efficiency.

Verify--Backtrack Loop: Structured Precondition Recovery for Long-Horizon Language Agents
Authors: Mingyang Liu, Qingcan Kang, Shixiong Kai, Tao Zhong, Mingxuan Yuan, Xin Li, Linqi Song
First: 2026-10-30T10:58:17+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T10:58:17+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Language agents increasingly tackle long-horizon tasks in interactive environments, where actions often depend on latent preconditions. The core difficulty is not trajectory length but recovery: when a prerequisite is missing or an earlier decomposition fails, trying another plausible action rarely helps. ReAct and later reflection or adaptive-planning methods support local correction, but express recovery as free-form text, with no explicit control state linking the terminal goal, the active goal, and the failure source. We introduce the Verify--Backtrack Loop (VBL), a training-free extension of ReAct that turns precondition discovery and failure diagnosis into structured planning-state updates. VBL maintains the task decomposition as a bounded goal stack and, on goal activation, execution failure, or material state change, jointly verifies two horizons---whether the active goal contributes to the terminal goal and whether it is feasible in the current state. These dual-horizon judgments, combined with typed failure attribution, trigger explicit operations---Push, Pop, retry, or BacktrackTo the parent. We evaluate VBL with Qwen3.5-9B, GPT-4o, and DeepSeek-V3.2 on AppWorld, ALFWorld, and ScienceWorld. VBL improves over ReAct and reflection-based control on every backbone--benchmark pair. On the challenging AppWorld setting, it improves task success by up to 22.07 percentage points over ReAct. Ablations show that removing either verification or diagnosis degrades performance, in several settings below ReAct, confirming that the two mechanisms are complementary. These results show that reliable long-horizon interaction benefits from revising the planning state, not merely the next action.
Summary / 总结
Language agents increasingly tackle long-horizon tasks in interactive environments, where actions often depend on latent preconditions.

Abstract
High-resolution 3D indoor scene generation is crucial for immersive applications such as virtual reality, spatial computing, and robotics simulation. We introduce VoxLution, a coarse-to-fine transformer-based approach that generates high-fidelity indoor scenes with rich details, overcoming the overly smooth geometry typical of existing generative models. First, we generate a low-resolution spatial layout; then, based on this structural information, we progressively refine the scene geometry locally in chunks. Key to our approach is formulating 3D super-resolution as a sequence modeling problem over multi-resolution sparse voxel grids. To achieve this, we introduce a training-free, resolution-invariant tokenization scheme that compactly encodes the geometric information of eight octants into a single decimal number. We formulate the transformer sequence to include multiple resolutions, allowing the coarse layout to guide the generation of fine local details. To ensure coherence across local chunks, we incorporate adjacent chunk boundary information directly into the transformer sequencing. VoxLution achieves scene generation at a finer voxel resolution than existing baselines, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance on standard benchmarks with 59.6% lower FPD and 51.1% lower FID on 3D-FRONT, and 70.5% lower FPD and 73.8% lower FID on Scannet++.

When Links Mislead: Label-Free Structural Polarity for Zero-Shot Classification of Web Pages
Authors: Hanshuai Cui, Zhiqing Tang, Zhi Yao, Weijia Jia, Jun Luo
First: 2026-10-30T09:16:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T09:16:42+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Web platforms such as digital libraries, online stores and university sites must classify new pages before anyone labels them. Large language models (LLMs) can label each page from its text, but these labels are noisy, and links could correct them only where linked pages share classes. Existing zero-shot methods ignore the links, or pull a page toward its neighbours as label propagation and graph neural networks (GNNs) do, which hurts where linked pages differ in class. Others weight propagation by an LLM-estimated homophily that costs extra calls and drifts across LLMs. We find that the sign with which links should enter a prediction, which we call structural polarity, is readable without true labels. It is the excess agreement of LLM labels and text-prototype predictions along links. We thus propose ZeroPolar, which gates one signed propagation step by this statistic. Training-free ZeroPolar mixes prototype scores with any number of LLM labels, and ZeroPolar-T denoises the labels with a text classifier once half of the pages carry one. With one GPT-4o label per page, ZeroPolar-T reaches the highest mean accuracy on eleven graphs (73.99%), 6.01 points above LLM-BP and significantly above all eighteen baselines we compare. With LLM labels on only 5% of the pages, training-free ZeroPolar leads a GNN trained on these labels by 10.80 points.

Abstract
Autonomous navigation in complex, unstructured outdoor environments requires robots to operate over long ranges without prior maps and limited depth sensing. In such settings, relying solely on geometric frontiers for exploration is often insufficient. In such settings, the ability to reason semantically about where to go and what is safe to traverse is crucial for robust, efficient exploration. This work presents WildOS, a unified system for long-range, open-vocabulary object search that combines safe geometric exploration with semantic visual reasoning. WildOS builds a sparse navigation graph to maintain spatial memory, while utilizing a foundation-model-based vision module, ExploRFM, to score frontier nodes of the graph. ExploRFM simultaneously predicts traversability, visual frontiers, and object similarity in image space, enabling real-time, onboard semantic navigation tasks. The resulting vision-scored graph enables the robot to explore semantically meaningful directions while ensuring geometric safety. Furthermore, we introduce a particle-filter-based method for coarse localization of the open-vocabulary target query, that estimates candidate goal positions beyond the robot's immediate depth horizon, enabling effective planning toward distant goals. Extensive closed-loop field experiments across diverse off-road and urban terrains demonstrate that WildOS enables robust navigation, significantly outperforming purely geometric and purely vision-based baselines in both efficiency and autonomy. Our results highlight the potential of vision foundation models to drive open-world robotic behaviors that are both semantically informed and geometrically grounded. Differential Vector Erasure: Unified Training-Free Concept Erasure for Flow Matching Models
Authors: Zhiqi Zhang, Xinhao Zhong, Yi Sun, Shuoyang Sun, Jiahui Wang, Bin Chen, Shu-Tao Xia, Xuan Wang

Abstract
Text-to-image diffusion models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in generating high-quality images, yet their tendency to reproduce undesirable concepts, such as NSFW content, copyrighted styles, or specific objects, poses growing concerns for safe and controllable deployment. While existing concept erasure approaches primarily focus on DDPM-based diffusion models and rely on costly fine-tuning, the recent emergence of flow matching models introduces a fundamentally different generative paradigm for which prior methods are not directly applicable. In this paper, we propose Differential Vector Erasure (DVE), a training-free concept erasure method specifically designed for flow matching models. Our key insight is that semantic concepts are implicitly encoded in the directional structure of the velocity field governing the generative flow. Leveraging this observation, we construct a differential vector field that characterizes the directional discrepancy between a target concept and a carefully chosen anchor concept. During inference, DVE selectively removes concept-specific components by projecting the velocity field onto the differential direction, enabling precise concept suppression without affecting irrelevant semantics. Extensive experiments on FLUX demonstrate that DVE consistently outperforms existing baselines on a wide range of concept erasure tasks, including NSFW suppression, artistic style removal, and object erasure, while preserving image quality and diversity. Code is available at https://github.com/zhiqizhang23/DVE.

Abstract
Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) fusion enables the composition of subject and style representations for controllable generation without retraining. However, existing approaches primarily operate through weight-level merging, without explicitly modeling how independently trained LoRAs interact in the shared parameter space. We adopt a geometric perspective on LoRA fusion, interpreting content and style LoRAs as occupying overlapping, non-orthogonal low-rank subspaces, where such overlap can lead to conflicting parameter updates that affect generation quality. This observation motivates us to reformulate LoRA fusion not merely as parameter combination, but as a problem of controlling how updates from overlapping subspaces are combined. Based on this insight, we propose Null Space Projection LoRA (NP-LoRA), a training-free framework that employs projection as a fusion operator to explicitly modulate cross-LoRA interactions. Specifically, NP-LoRA uses principal directions of the style LoRA to define a projection subspace and projects the content LoRA onto the complementary subspace (i.e., the null space of the style LoRA), suppressing interference along dominant style directions while preserving complementary information. To avoid the overly aggressive suppression of hard projection, we further formulate soft projection as a regularized optimization problem that balances content preservation against style-subspace suppression. This objective admits a closed-form solution, yielding a projection operator controlled by a single parameter that continuously interpolates between linear merging and hard projection. Extensive experiments across multiple pretrained LoRA pairs show that NP-LoRA achieves more balanced content-style composition compared to strong baselines, without requiring retraining. Code is available at https://github.com/blackzebra7/NP-LoRA.

Abstract
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities across vision-language tasks, yet their large-scale deployment raises pressing concerns about memorized private data, outdated knowledge, and harmful content. Existing unlearning approaches for MLLMs typically adapt training-based strategies such as gradient ascent or preference optimization, but these methods are computationally expensive, irreversible, and often distort retained knowledge. In this work, we propose MLLMEraser, an input-aware, training-free framework for test-time unlearning. Our approach leverages activation steering to enable dynamic knowledge erasure without parameter updates. Specifically, we construct a multimodal erasure direction by contrasting adversarially perturbed, knowledge-recall image-text pairs with knowledge-erasure counterparts, capturing both textual and visual discrepancies. To prevent unnecessary interference, we further design an input-aware steering mechanism that adaptively determines when and how the erasure direction should be applied, preserving utility on retained knowledge while enforcing forgetting on designated content. Experiments on LLaVA-1.5 and Qwen-2.5-VL demonstrate that MLLMEraser consistently outperforms state-of-the-art MLLM unlearning baselines, achieving stronger forgetting performance with lower computational cost and minimal utility degradation.

Abstract
Spatial reasoning in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has increasingly benefited from tool-integrated pipelines that ground computation in explicit geometry. However, existing systems often compile queries into relatively open-ended artifacts, such as code, task constraints, tool plans, or gathered evidence, leaving what must be resolved before execution under-specified. This can lead to unnecessary tool acquisition and iterative refinement, while still producing plausible reasoning traces from incomplete spatial commitments. We identify this failure mode as under-committed query compilation. To address it, we propose PEPS, a training-free agentic paradigm Parsing Executable Primitives for Spatial reasoning under a typed FESM schema over Frame, Entity, State, and Metric. PEPS follows a parse-before-execute principle: the Parser commits each query to a compact set of executable requirements; the Executor selectively grounds the required primitives through fixed geometric APIs and deterministic computation; and the Verifier checks execution sufficiency, enabling early termination or targeted repair. Extensive experiments across challenging spatial reasoning benchmarks show that PEPS consistently improves reasoning accuracy while substantially reducing refinement rounds, API cost, and end-to-end latency over strong tool-integrated baselines. These results demonstrate that explicit requirement commitment can make tool-integrated spatial reasoning not only more reliable, but also more execution-efficient.

Abstract
Training-free expert pruning makes two decisions: which experts to remove, and what the layer computes when its router still selects a removed expert. Published methods change both at once. Holding the mask fixed, we compare replacement operators from three families on OLMoE, DeepSeek-V2-Lite and Moonlight, with Granite as a fourth router, using paired, multiplicity-corrected tests. The largest effect is a choice usually left implicit: the survivors' gate scale. On routers trained without top-k renormalization, renormalizing the survivors' gates, a common implementation choice, costs 0.8-8.5 points and reverses whether rerouting beats MoNE's mean-output replacement; on two routers trained with renormalization, the trained scale wins at 50% pruning. We introduce the tilted novice, a closed-form low-rank map of the pruned expert's input whose rank-0 case is MoNE's constant. At 50% pruning it adds 1.2-1.8% parameters and gains 3.45 and 1.37 points over the constant on OLMoE and Moonlight, stable across calibration draws and largely unchanged by 4- and 3-bit quantization. No operator dominates: trained-scale rerouting is best on DeepSeek-V2-Lite, and a partial copy of each pruned expert is statistically indistinguishable from the tilted novice on two models. Evaluation can change conclusions: at 25% pruning MoNE's constant keeps 92-97% of dense multiple-choice accuracy but only 5-65% of GSM8K and 0-17% of code scores, and the calibration domain moves the mask 4-9 times more than resampling. We propose a five-item reporting checklist.

Abstract
Lightweight vision-language models perform competitively on standard benchmarks yet fail systematically in dense-scene reasoning, where multiple objects, attributes, and relations must be jointly grounded and resolved through multi-step inference. Such capability is critical for real-world applications where models must reliably interpret cluttered environments. Yet existing training signals provide no explicit grounding between reasoning steps and the underlying visual entities and relations, leaving lightweight models free to generate fluent but visually unanchored reasoning chains. To address this gap, we first introduce DRBench, a benchmark of 14,573 questions across 2,943 images, organized into five task categories spanning three progressive reasoning layers. Abstract

Why VLMs Miss Small Objects, and When Zooming In Is Safe
Authors: Junzhe Shi, Yuan Gan, Shida Jiang
First: 2026-10-07T02:10:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T06:22:53+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Screenshots, documents, engineering drawings, shelves and maps hold many small objects of kinds that change from task to task, and vision-language models (VLMs) promise to find them from an instruction and an example. Yet they see each image through a fixed token budget and miss small objects, so every user, and every agent that calls a model, must choose whether to send an image whole, raise its resolution, split it, or zoom. We show that VLMs miss small objects for two reasons a caller can estimate before any call: an object gets too few visual tokens (S), and a call must search and report too much (L). Raising S by a factor a multiplies the tokens needed to see an image by a^2, while splitting at the same scale lowers L at a cost at most linear in the number of views. Under two monotonicity assumptions, splitting cannot lower expected recall if no view zooms out and views overlap by one object, and neither part can be dropped; every such split pays a minimum token cost, and a simple rule pays it up to an explicit factor. Better models need less S, but an image can be arbitrarily large while a call's budget is fixed, so splitting does not become obsolete, for zoom agents either. In about 187,000 requests to 12 VLMs, up to GPT-6.1, Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 4, on controlled images 24 of 40 predicted orderings hold significantly and none is violated; splits that never zoom out raised recall on average over models wherever S was below 1.5, and splits that zoom out lowered it in 19 of 21 model and data settings. The rule raised recall significantly in five of ten settings, by up to 0.28 (from 0.62 to 0.90), and precision and F1 in six, at a token cost known before any call.

Abstract
Long-horizon LLM agents play an important role in solving complex and sustained tasks, with ever-growing interaction contexts. However, these accumulated contexts will raise substantial computational cost and obscure key decision-relevant evidence. Existing methods typically train external compressors or context-management agents to rewrite context as shorter hard-token sequences, but such external selection may not align with the LLM's evolving representation and is necessarily lossy. In this paper, we introduce Agentic Soft-Token Compression (ASTC), a training-free compression method using soft tokens within LLM agent for long-horizon tasks. Specifically, ASTC uses the model's native recurrent dynamics to determine the already absorbed information, leveraging the commonly-used hybrid Gated Delta Network (GDN)-attention. It then carries this information forward as agentic soft tokens while retaining exact values as hard tokens. The resulting compact sequence reduces later-layer computation and yields reusable KV caches, extending the savings across the agent's trajectory. Extensive experiments on long-horizon tasks show that ASTC matches or exceeds the uncompressed agent's accuracy with up to 50.68% fewer end-to-end Attention Pairs, while trained compressors require 15.4 to 103.7 times more Attention Pairs per successful task. These findings highlight agentic soft tokens as a model-native way to carry absorbed context forward as the task representation evolves.

Abstract
GUI grounding maps natural-language instructions to click locations and is essential for reliable GUI agents. The task remains difficult on high-resolution, densely populated interfaces because a vision-language model (VLM) may recognize a requested control without locating it precisely enough for interaction. Most existing methods provide various forms of localization assistance, but still rely on a direct click prediction, allowing visual ambiguity or an inaccurate initial estimate to propagate to the final result. In this paper, we introduce GUI-Lens, a coarse-to-fine grounding framework that allows a general-purpose VLM to determine the target through active visual observations. Specifically, GUI-Lens extracts OCR text and detected UI components from the screenshot and presents their positions as coordinate references. Using the instruction, the current view, and these references, the VLM selects the region and scale of the next view, which is cropped and enlarged to provide finer visual details. This process continues over successively focused views until the target is determined. Proposed crops and clicks are checked against the instruction throughout the process, and the final local position is mapped back to the original screen coordinates. Experiments on five GUI grounding benchmarks and six general-purpose base models show that GUI-Lens consistently improves grounding accuracy, achieving up to 9.3 times the accuracy of direct grounding on ScreenSpot-Pro.

Abstract

Abstract
Recent large vision-language models (LVLMs) can generate vision-text multimodal chain-of-thought (MCoT) traces after reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT). However, we observe that the visual information in MCoT is often inaccurate even when answers are correct, and task accuracy remains nearly unchanged despite the visual information being corrupted. This indicates a lack of faithfulness in the vision part of MCoT reasoning. We attribute this to the RL reward design in RFT, which solely incentivizes the format of interleaved vision-text cues, encouraging the model to incorporate visual information into its text reasoning steps without considering its correctness. In this paper, we first probe MCoT faithfulness by measuring how much the prediction changes when its visual and textual thoughts are intervened. Surprisingly, the model's predictions remain nearly unchanged under visual intervention but change significantly under textual intervention, indicating that the visual evidence is largely ignored. To further analyze the visual information, we introduce an automated LVLM-based evaluation metric that quantifies the faithfulness of visual cues from two perspectives: relevance and sufficiency. Our evaluation reveals that the visual information in current MCoT traces is simultaneously irrelevant and insufficient. To address this issue, we propose Sufficient-Component Cause Model (SCCM) learning. This approach encourages the MCoT to generate sufficient yet minimal visual components that are independently capable of leading to the correct answer. The proposed SCCM is annotation-free and plug-and-play, compatible with various RFT for MCoT. Empirical results demonstrate that SCCM substantially improves the visual faithfulness across a suite of fine-grained perception and reasoning benchmarks. Code is available at https://github.com/EugeneLiu01/Faithful_Thinking_with_Image.

Sensorimotor Alignment: A Platonic Proxy for Embodied Policy Success
Authors: Yunfei Xie, Howard H. Qian, Shiyi Lan, Kaiyu Hang, Chen Wei
First: 2026-10-22T17:59:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:59:51+00:00
Comments: 28 pages. Project page and code available at https://yunfeixie233.github.io/s2/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Robotic foundation models are typically evaluated through costly closed-loop rollouts, which motivates cheap offline proxies for model capability. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) suggests that as representations improve, different modalities such as vision and language converge toward a shared statistical model of reality. We ask whether motion trajectories form another such modality: as embodied models become more capable, should their sensory representations converge toward the structure of physical motion? To examine this, we propose Sensorimotor Alignment ($S^2$), a training-free offline metric that measures how well a model's sensory representation aligns with the geometry of physical motion. We find that $S^2$ correlates positively with downstream robot task success across embodied model families, making it a low-cost offline proxy for prioritizing models before closed-loop evaluation. $S^2$ can also identify promising vision-language model (VLM) backbones even before costly fine-tuning into vision-language-action models (VLAs). Beyond this correlation, $S^2$ offers representation-level explanations for phenomena observed in VLAs and world action models (WAMs). With $S^2$, we extend PRH to embodied models and make sensorimotor alignment empirically measurable, offering insight into why an embodied model succeeds beyond its closed-loop success rate.

WorldForge V2: Eliciting Camera-Trajectory Alignment in Video Diffusion Models
Authors: Chenxi Song, Yanming Yang, Tong Zhao, Ruibo Li, Joey Tianyi Zhou, Chi Zhang
First: 2026-10-22T17:53:03+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:53:03+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Video diffusion models have learned rich 3D/4D priors, making them promising for scene generation and video re-rendering. However, their use in spatial tasks is limited by imprecise camera control, spatial and temporal inconsistencies, and entangled camera motion with scene dynamics. Existing approaches, such as task-specific fine-tuning and post-process warping, often introduce visual artifacts, generalize poorly, or incur high computational costs. We propose WorldForge V2 to achieve accurate camera control while preserving the generative capabilities of pretrained video models. We first develop a training-free guidance framework using warped references to iteratively refine generation along a target camera path. Optical-flow analysis identifies motion-related latent channels for selective guidance, helping separate motion control from appearance. A dual-path strategy further compares guided and unguided predictions to correct artifacts from imperfect references. Together, these components guide viewpoint changes while preserving scene appearance and object motion. To reduce refinement costs, v-cache approximates the correction using the preceding denoising prediction, avoiding an additional model evaluation. We further extend motion localization from latent channels to network blocks and introduce Trajectory LoRA, which concentrates adaptation on blocks most responsive to trajectory guidance. A two-stage training strategy teaches these adapters to denoise the states encountered during guided generation, using limited supervision effectively. This enables reliable camera control with very little training data, with a single training scene already supporting generalization to unseen scenes. Quantitative and qualitative evaluations across multiple datasets show that our method achieves state-of-the-art generation quality and trajectory accuracy in camera-controlled video generation.

Abstract
Large language models have shown outstanding performance on text-first tasks such as coding, math, and writing. Recent efforts are shifting models' training to include scientific problems that often live in highly specialized domains beyond text, such as protein structure and biological images. However, in these scenarios, their general-purpose accuracy compared with specialized algorithms remains in question. One such domain is cell tracking, which requires spatiotemporal awareness of cell dynamics. To evaluate this, we created a new benchmark, CellTrack-Bench, which assesses Vision-Language Models' (VLMs) cell-tracking-related performance against specialized trackers. The full benchmark draws from 15 standard cell-tracking datasets across four imaging modalities and comprises 1,537 problems corresponding to 9,041 cell-linking decisions. We selected a hard subset requiring greater reasoning effort by identifying failures shared across distinct algorithmic trackers. With this proposed dataset, we: (i) evaluated different environment harnesses for VLMs; (ii) benchmarked 9 frontier closed-source models and 13 open-weight models; and (iii) provided a detailed analysis of their failure modes. Code, the stored model outputs, and a pipeline that regenerates the benchmark from the source datasets are available at https://github.com/royerlab/celltrack-bench.

Squaring-the-Sphere: Distortion-Free and Seamless Panorama Generation via CubeAtlas
Authors: Xuhui Zuo, Chenjie Cao, Tengfei Wang, Junta Wu, Yisu Zhang, Chunchao Guo
First: 2026-10-22T17:06:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:06:55+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Generating 360-degree panoramas requires both local perspective fidelity and global spherical continuity. Existing representations face a trade-off: equirectangular projection (ERP) enables seamless panorama modeling but suffers from severe distortion and a domain gap to pre-trained image generators, whereas cubemaps preserve perspective geometry but often produce inconsistent faces and visible seams. We present Squaring-the-Sphere, a panoramic image generation framework built on a novel representation CubeAtlas, which unfolds all cubemap faces into a single compact canvas. By arranging orientation-aligned, rotated polar regions alongside the equatorial faces, this representation preserves the perspective geometry of cubemaps while organizing the cross-face relationships required for seamless panorama assembly. It further enables a pretrained diffusion transformer to model the complete panorama jointly in a single pass, without altering the backbone architecture or positional encoding. While CubeAtlas has a token count comparable to ERP, its structured layout makes many dense attention interactions unnecessary. We therefore introduce a layout-aware sparse attention mask that preserves the interactions required for panorama continuity while removing geometrically irrelevant token pairs. As a result, CubeAtlas achieves better quality than ERP modeling with lower attention computation. Furthermore, we introduce a training-free super-resolution technique and a fusion scheme that enhance fine details in distortion-free perspective space before achieving the final ERP panorama. Experiments on various benchmarks demonstrate that our method improves geometric fidelity, cross-view consistency, and high-resolution visual quality over previous methods.

Abstract
Subject-driven personalized text-to-image systems commonly generate multiple candidates but return a single output, yet the selection stage is rarely evaluated as an explicit system component. Identity-only selection may overemphasize reference similarity at the expense of prompt alignment and output diversity. We propose distribution-calibrated candidate selection (DCAL), a training-free sequential selector for a predefined ordered prompt set that jointly scores calibrated identity, text alignment, and cross-prompt novelty. All selectors operate on identical pre-generated K=4 candidate pools for all 30 DreamBooth subjects using a fixed LoRA-adapted generator. Relative to the fixed-seed Random-of-4 control, DCAL yields higher 1-LPIPS, CLIP-I, DINO-I, and CLIP-T for 22, 25, 28, and 22 of the 30 subjects, respectively. Compared with identity-only selection, DCAL improves CLIP-T (0.3264 versus 0.3179), CLIP diversity (0.2444 versus 0.2368), and DINO diversity (0.3286 versus 0.3170), while CLIP-I and DINO-I decrease from 0.8250 to 0.8204 and from 0.7560 to 0.7453. Compared with identity-text scoring, prompt-wise novelty calibration increases CLIP-T from 0.3253 to 0.3264 and CLIP diversity from 0.2366 to 0.2444. We further formalize finite-pool Pareto and perturbation-stability properties and analyze inference complexity.

Abstract
Coordinating two players in a cooking task requires a vision-language agent to track changing scene states, sequence recipe steps, and allocate shared resources under a time budget. We introduce an Overcooked-inspired benchmark for evaluating these capabilities in Unreal Engine environments constructed using coding agents. A single vision-language model controls both players from top-down visual observations, selecting high-level actions while the engine executes navigation. The benchmark contains 20 maps in five categories. These categories support evaluation of centralized coordination under equipment contention, changing terrain, and handoff dependencies. A prop legend and pot ingredient-count indicators provide explicit visual cues, while grid-based destinations support spatial grounding. The evaluation compares four VLMs on all 20 maps, measuring task score, order completion, delivery precision, action acceptance rate, and model API expenditure. A memory ablation on the same 20 maps examines the effect of a shared, self-authored notebook while retaining six-response action history in both conditions. The strongest evaluated model, \texttt{gpt-6-astra}, achieves 26.2\% mean order completion. Notebook memory has mixed score effects across models, with positive mean differences for Claude and Qwen and negative differences for GPT and Gemini.

Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) can perform a broad range of tasks across diverse settings, yet their performance in agentic contexts remains poorly understood. Although online evaluations in simulators and real-world environments are essential for measuring interactive agent performance, they are costly and challenging to scale. Complementary offline VQA benchmarks can enable faster and more reliable comparisons across agentic domains, yet existing benchmarks remain limited and fragmented. To address this gap, we introduce AgentVQA, an offline benchmark for evaluating VLMs on realistic agentic scenarios across five agent domains: Web Agents, Robotics, Egocentric Videos, Games, and Spatial Planning. We build AgentVQA by manually selecting 1000 challenging, real-world agent examples from 23 source datasets and adapting them for reliable offline evaluation. The resulting benchmark preserves realistic scenarios from interactive environments across diverse domains, and its model rankings track those from corresponding online evaluations. Across the 22 evaluated models, the best achieves only 58.5% accuracy. Our cross-domain analysis shows that errors are driven primarily by failures to ground and maintain accurate representations of the current and evolving visual state, including localizing relevant UI elements, interpreting motion, tracking state changes over time, and inferring 3D spatial relationships, rather than by task misinterpretation or downstream logical reasoning.

Abstract
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have shown strong potential for video anomaly detection, but dense frame-level inference makes them impractical for real-time deployment. Existing acceleration commonly relies on uniform sampling, which ignores content dynamics and can either waste computation on static segments or miss localized anomalies. We propose MoG-VLM a motion-adaptive anomaly detection framework that leverages motion purely as a lightweight control signal to decide when VLM inference is necessary. To this end, we introduce Temporal Gradient Magnitude (TGM) which performs content-aware sampling to concentrate computation on informative regions while avoiding redundant processing. We further introduce a hierarchical dual-stream design in which dense multi-frame VLM reasoning is activated only around frames with elevated anomaly likelihood, via anchor-triggered context-window expansion with early termination. A fusion module then reconciles these instantaneous semantic cues with temporal action dynamics. Across four benchmarks, our method achieves 91.91%, 84.30%, 87.88%, and 78.96% AUC-ROC on XD-Violence, UCF-Crime, MSAD, and ShanghaiTech, respectively, establishing new state of the art on three benchmarks under the zero-shot, training-free setting. On XD-Violence, we improve over the previous best result by +0.57% while delivering a 1.31x inference speedup. These results demonstrate that content-aware allocation of VLM capacity, processing fewer frames more intelligently rather than all frames uniformly, is a more effective axis of progress than uniformly densifying VLM inference across frames. Code is available on GitHub: https://github.com/kdawoud91/MoG-VLM.

Authors: Di Yang, Xiaochang Li, Muyang Liu, Yufei Wang, Yanhai Xiong
First: 2026-10-22T15:59:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T15:59:59+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Many questions about a video concern one physical object. Where does the ball end up after the cups are shuffled? Which card is the queen? How often was the mug covered? Video-language models (Video-LMs) recognise such objects in single frames but do not keep them apart over time. On real shell-game videos, open models from 8B to 235B parameters, their thinking variants, a reinforcement-learned tool agent and three training-free tool agents all stay within ten points of chance. We separate semantic identity binding from temporal identity maintenance. An object's identity should be fixed at a moment when the evidence for it is visible, and carried afterwards by a tracker. \emph{Track, Then Bind} implements this principle without training. A promptable video tracker follows every candidate object and draws it with a track number. A frozen vision-language model answers a static question about the identifying evidence, which binds the target to a track. The answer is then derived from the tracked state in text, without asking the model to reinterpret the original video during read-out. A single configuration covers all benchmarks. With Qwen3-VL-32B it solves both VET-Bench tasks, where a fine-tuned trajectory model reaches 92\% and 90\% and the same VLM voting over 16 samples stays at 30--32\%. It answers 55.4\% of the hardest Perception Test cup games, where the best published model answers 41.5\%, and, using track-derived timelines, 48.6\% of TOC-Bench questions, against 37.1\% for the same model answering directly. Drawing the same tracks on the video and asking the question directly falls 12--19 points short of the agent on real cup games.

A Sparse Topometric Graph for Scalable Cross-Session Indoor Navigation
Authors: Eun Sun Lee, Minkyun Seo, Gwangtak Bae, Junho Kim, Seunggu Kang, Jaesik Park, Young Min Kim
First: 2026-10-22T15:30:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T15:30:33+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A conventional embodied agent in an indoor environment builds a map by accumulating noisy real-world measurements and results from heavy inference to guide navigation. While the detailed measurements may differ due to scene changes or sensor settings, the unique topological structure of navigable passages or the connectivity between rooms may provide training-free guidance that complements local observations. We propose a sparse topometric graph as an efficient structural representation for robust indoor navigation, alleviating the need for fine-grained matching, which is computationally expensive, memory-intensive, and prone to real-world variations. The map consists of nodes that encode local 3D geometry and distance-weighted edges that capture traversable connectivity. A learned graph-matching network finds consistent subgraph structures across sessions by considering both node-level geometric descriptors and neighborhood connectivity. The holistic contextual matching is robust to local ambiguities caused by weak distinctiveness or repetitive structure, and establishes stable correspondences despite local scene changes, different sensor modalities, or limited spatial overlap. We analyze the practicality of the graph through cross-session point-goal navigation, where a goal specified in a prior reference map must be reached from an unknown initial pose in a later session. Experiments show that our method achieves the highest success rates (77.1% on Gibson and 40.0% under 3D-FRONT scene changes), while reducing memory by more than two orders of magnitude compared to point-level representations. The proposed approach further supports cross-session alignment and goal-directed navigation with strong scalability and robust real-world transfer.

Training-free Acceleration for Blind Compressed Image Restoration via Activation-Calibrated Singular Value Decomposition
Authors: Suhang Yao, Xin Li, Xijun Wang, Yufei Wang, Yeying Jin, Zhibo Chen
First: 2026-10-21T16:16:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T15:23:22+00:00
Comments: 11 pages. Accepted for publication in IEEE Journal on Emerging and Selected Topics in Circuits and Systems
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Transformer-based models achieve strong performance on blind compressed image restoration (CIR) under unknown compression settings, but their densely stacked projection operators make deployment costly. We study training-free acceleration for such models through an empirical, operator-level analysis. Specifically, we profile where computation is concentrated, characterize channel-wise activation distributions under compressed inputs, and examine the singular-value decay of representative projection weights. These measurements identify the recurring projections in the attention, feed-forward, convolutional, and prompt-conditioning pathways as effective low-rank replacement targets that preserve the original inference graph. They further establish truncated SVD (TSVD) as a principled training-free baseline for blind CIR and reveal why a uniform low-rank objective is mismatched to the channel-imbalanced feature distributions of compressed images. Accordingly, we formulate Activation-Calibrated Singular Value Decomposition (ASVD) for this restoration setting, deriving low-rank factors entirely offline from pretrained weights and lightweight activation statistics gathered from only a few compressed calibration images. By weighting the decomposition with channel-wise activation magnitudes, our ASVD assigns larger penalties to errors on more active channels, requiring neither retraining nor online calibration and leaving the restoration pipeline intact. When instantiated on PromptCIR at rank 64, ASVD consistently improves PSNR-B over TSVD across four standard benchmarks under identical parameter and MAC budgets. At this operating point, the factorized model cuts parameters by 59% (34.75M to 14.34M) and computation by 16.1% (258.79 to 217.16 GMACs for a 256 x 256 input), enabling acceleration without retraining.

Authors: Aniket Anand, Janvijay Singh, Zhewei Sun, Dilek Hakkani-Tür, Nick Feamster

Abstract
Post-trained language models often exhibit a recognizable AI-like writing style, yet its connection to post-training and internal representations remains poorly understood. In this work, we study whether post-training introduces or amplifies AI-like stylistic regularities and whether these regularities have a localized internal signature. To this end, we compare human text, base-model generations, and aligned-model generations under matched human-source prefixes. Aligned generations show lower human-corpus affinity and higher AI-detection rates than base generations, suggesting that post-training shifts generated text away from human-corpus style and toward detector-visible AI-like text. We then introduce PASTA (Post-training Alignment Signature Targeted Ablation), a training-free method that estimates a post-training alignment signature from aligned-base residual contrasts and ablates the corresponding direction during decoding. Across 11 aligned models and 6 AI-detectors, PASTA lowers the detection rate for most aligned models; this effect transfers well across detectors and is not reproduced by random directions. Qualitative analysis suggests that PASTA generations remain relevant and coherent while exhibiting greater stylistic variation. Together, these results show that AI-like stylistic effects of post-training can be measured, localized, and causally tested through activation ablation.

Abstract
The performance of machine learning models depends on discovering features in the data that are relevant to solving the given task while ignoring irrelevant variation. To check and visualize whether this is the case, we propose 'invariance auditing', a diagnostic tool to analyze feature extractors. Invariance auditing generates diverse inputs which all yield the same features as a given query input, and are therefore treated as identical by the model. Using external knowledge about the task at hand, this enables assessing if the learned invariances do or do not conform to the task's required semantics. Unlike existing work where a dedicated generative model is trained for each feature extractor, our algorithm is training-free and exploits a pretrained diffusion or flow-matching model to sample invariant inputs. Our fiber loss -- which penalizes feature mismatch -- guides the denoising process so the output shares the same representation as the query input. This replaces days of training with a single guided generation procedure at comparable quality. Experiments on popular datasets and model types demonstrate that our auditing method reveals invariances spanning very desirable and concerning behavior. For instance, it detects cases where Qwen-2B places patients with situs inversus (heart on the right side) in the same fiber as typical anatomy. Our code is available at https://github.com/vislearn/InvarianceAuditing.

CountZES: Counting via Zero-Shot Exemplar Discovery and Selection
Authors: Muhammad Ibraheem Siddiqui, Muhammad Haris Khan
First: 2025-12-18T11:12:50+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T14:23:54+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Zero-shot object counting (ZOC) aims to count instances of unseen categories using only a class name. Existing exemplar-based methods discover candidate exemplars through random image patches or open-vocabulary detector proposals. However, random patch sampling often yields poorly delineated instances, while detector-based discovery may produce multi-instance regions and, in dense scenes, provide only a few or even no suitable single-instance candidates. Thus, exemplar selection is constrained by candidate quality and coverage, while counting itself remains highly sensitive to exemplar choice. To this end, we propose CountZES, an inference-only framework that jointly addresses candidate discovery and exemplar selection through density-guided candidate generation and complementary exemplar-selection mechanisms. Starting from a text-grounded anchor, CountZES progressively discovers and refines visual exemplars through three stages: Detection-Anchored Exemplar (DAE), Density-Guided Exemplar (DGE), and Feature-Consensus Exemplar (FCE). DAE establishes a reliable single-instance anchor, DGE leverages density responses to expand candidate discovery beyond the detector-constrained pool and select a statistically consistent exemplar, and FCE promotes representativeness through feature-level consensus. Together, they yield complementary exemplars balancing semantic grounding, count consistency, and feature representativeness. CountZES requires no ZOC-specific optimization and adopts a modular design that supports different pretrained components. Extensive evaluations across natural, aerial, and medical domains demonstrate strong cross-domain generalization and superior performance among inference-only ZOC methods.

Abstract
4D scene reconstruction requires robust handling of dynamic objects, yet existing streaming methods commonly assume static scenes, leading to pose drift and ghosting artifacts over long sequences. We introduce Stream4D, a training-free, motion-decoupled streaming reconstruction framework that can be directly integrated into causal Transformer backbones (e.g., StreamVGGT, Stream3R). Abstract

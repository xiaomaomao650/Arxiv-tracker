CoSpaDi: Compressing LLMs via Calibration-Guided Sparse Dictionary Learning
Authors: Denis Makhov, Dmitriy Shopkhoev, Magauiya Zhussip, Ammar Ali, Stamatios Lefkimmiatis
First: 2025-09-26T08:55:09+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T17:53:03+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Post-training LLM compression often relies on low-rank approximations, which force all columns of a projection matrix to share a single low-dimensional subspace. We propose CoSpaDi, a training-free compression framework that replaces this single-subspace assumption with a union-of-subspaces model via sparse dictionary learning. CoSpaDi factorizes each weight matrix into a dense dictionary and column-sparse coefficients, allowing different columns to select different subsets of dictionary atoms at the same storage budget. To preserve model behavior, we use calibration activations to transform functional reconstruction into a standard dictionary learning problem. Across LLaMA and Qwen models, CoSpaDi improves accuracy-compression and perplexity-compression trade-offs over SVD-based and structured pruning baselines at 20-40\% compression ratios, while naturally supporting sparse-dense computation and post-training quantization of sparse coefficients.

Abstract
A robot policy needs to understand what a task is asking for in a scene, and how to move the robot to accomplish that task. To achieve these two skills, we introduce Zero-real Agent Policy (ZAP), a recipe that teaches a small off-the-shelf vision-language model to control a robot via a simple tool harness. We use a pretrained 27B parameter model to bootstrap world understanding and reasoning, followed by supervised fine-tuning and GRPO on simple simulated tasks to teach it how to move the robot. Without any real-world robot data during training, ZAP reaches 96.7% success on real-world tasks, matching GPT-6 Astra in our harness and outperforming the MolmoAct2 VLA (33.3%), while running 3x faster and 17x cheaper than GPT-6 Astra. We demonstrate that ZAP also generalizes to completely new objects, goals, strategies, and language by seating a test tube in a rack (and choosing a different hole in the rack when prompted), handing a test tube between arms mid-air, drawing an X on a tic-tac-toe board, and playing chess. These goals and types of manipulation don't appear in our training data. Furthermore, ZAP also transfers to an unseen embodiment. On a single-arm Franka, ZAP reaches 55.9% on RoboLab-120, 4.9 percentage points ahead of the leading VLA/WAM baseline.

VidEdit3D: Flexible 3D Gaussian Editing with Video Diffusion
Authors: Yanming Yang, Chenxi Song, Tong Zhao, Juntao Xu, Wenhao Rao, Yiwen Chen, Yiwei Wang, Chi Zhang
First: 2026-10-26T17:03:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T17:03:44+00:00
Comments: Project Page: https://2hitee.github.io/VidEdit3D-page/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Recent advancements in text-guided 3D editing, particularly with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), have shown promise but still lag behind the flexibility of 2D editing tools. Existing methods face a critical trade-off between preserving multi-view consistency and allowing structural modifications. Image-based editors can also struggle to apply the intended edit to objects viewed from unusual angles. To address these limitations, we introduce VidEdit3D, a framework that reformulates 3D scene editing as a trajectory-based video editing task. By drawing a parallel between camera movement in static 3D scenes and temporal dynamics in video, we leverage the robust spatiotemporal priors of pre-trained Video Diffusion Models (VDMs) to encourage semantic and structural coherence across views. Specifically, we propose Trajectory-Guided Rendering (TGR) to select informative keyframes and construct smooth video clips aligned with VDM input requirements. To propagate edits from an anchor view to these clips, we develop Flow-Aligned Video Editing (FAVE), a training-free framework incorporating an Editing Consistency Guidance (ECG) mechanism. ECG contrasts source and edited image conditions at a shared latent state, using scheduled source-image amplification to strengthen adherence to the edited reference while maintaining original scene context. Finally, we introduce Region-Selective 3DGS Refinement (RS-GS) to strictly confine geometric and appearance updates to target primitives, reducing background attribute leakage. Experiments demonstrate stronger instruction alignment and better multi-view consistency than the baselines.

Triage: Skip, Skim, or Refine Attention Tiles in Video Diffusion Transformers
Authors: Yeonjae Kim, Omin Kwon, Seong Hoon Seo, Jae W. Lee
First: 2026-10-26T16:29:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T16:29:36+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Attention over tens of thousands of visual tokens dominates the inference of video diffusion transformers (DiTs). Existing methods reduce this cost through sparsity, low precision, or both, but use a single precision for all kept tiles. We observe that the output error introduced by approximation varies widely across tiles. Many tiles can be skipped with little error, others must be computed but gain little beyond low precision, and a few retain substantial error even at low precision. We present Triage, a training-free attention method that assigns each tile one of three approximation tiers: skipping it by replacing it with its block means, skimming it in NVFP4, or refining it with NVFP4 residual correction. A lightweight router predicts each tile's error under each tier from block statistics and moves a tile to a more accurate tier only when the predicted error reduction justifies the extra compute, with a single price setting this trade-off. A fused kernel executes all three tiers in a single pass on the FP4 tensor cores of RTX Blackwell GPUs. Across Wan2.2, HunyuanVideo, and LTX-2.5, Triage accelerates end-to-end generation by up to 2.32x over dense FlashAttention, and in each of five settings it runs faster than every baseline while reaching 0.3-3.0 dB higher PSNR than the highest-PSNR baseline.

Abstract

Abstract
Vision Language Models (VLMs) have shown impressive capabilities in high-level reasoning and task planning for robots. However, specialized domains remain a challenge for VLMs' image interpretation and grounding abilities. We introduce TrySee, a modular closed-loop robot task planning pipeline integrating general-purpose VLM reasoning with domain-specific perception and established robotics components. The system consists of an inherently reactive cyclic planning pipeline, augmented with domain-specific modular vision preprocessing, to provide online grounding. Finally, a dynamic preprocessing selection mechanism contributes to the scalability of the pipeline. The system requires neither VLM fine-tuning nor end-to-end demonstrations and adapts through replaceable perception modules and motion planning. The approach is evaluated on planning tasks across 5 widely different domains, including a real-world robot disassembly task. The field tests in a brownfield environment demonstrate the approach's practicality in integrating VLM planning into specialized robotic systems with limited domain-specific training data.

Abstract
Modern text-to-image models produce impressive visual results from richly specified prompts, yet their behavior under long prompts remains insufficiently understood. In this paper, we study a practical failure mode in which accumulated semantic constraints progressively suppress output variation, causing diversity to collapse even when many visual factors remain unspecified. We show that this phenomenon appears consistently across recent generation models as prompt length increases, and provide a theoretical motivation that connects long-prompt conditioning with reduced sampling entropy in the prompt embedding space. Based on this observation, we introduce PromptMoG, a training-free approach that samples prompt embeddings from a Mixture-of-Gaussians distribution to restore generative flexibility while maintaining semantic fidelity. To support systematic evaluation, we further present LPD-Bench, a structured benchmark of long and semantically dense prompts for measuring both fidelity and diversity under compositional text conditioning. Extensive experiments on four large-scale diffusion models, including SD3.5-Large, Flux.1-Krea-Dev, CogView4, and Qwen-Image, show that PromptMoG consistently improves diversity for long-prompt image generation. ArchiveLens: A Generative Spatial Knowledge Framework for Semantic Reconstruction of Historical Maps
Authors: Yanan Wu, Yalin Yang, Chengbin Deng
First: 2026-10-26T15:29:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T15:29:24+00:00
Comments: 31 pages

Abstract
Historical maps contain dense morphological and semantic information about past urban environments, but many digitization workflows focus primarily on extracting geometry, and do not capture cartographic semantics such as building use and construction materials. This study introduces ArchiveLens, a generative spatial knowledge framework that operationalizes historical maps as entity-centric, machine-queryable geospatial databases. The pipeline decouples morphological and semantic reconstruction through three stages: tiled YOLOv11-seg instance segmentation for building footprint extraction; deterministic material classification via hue, saturation, and value (HSV)-based color thresholds; and localized attribute parsing using a quantized vision-language model constrained by domain-specific prompts. Extracted entities are integrated into a hybrid Spatial-Temporal Retrieval-Augmented Generation (ST-RAG) architecture that combines dense semantic retrieval with symbolic spatio-temporal filtering and mandatory provenance grounding. Applied to 445 georeferenced Sanborn map sheets of Chicago (1918-1950), the framework achieves high geometric and semantic accuracy. Beyond performance, ArchiveLens advances GIScience by formalizing historical cartography as structured spatial knowledge representation, allowing researchers to query building attributes and compare spatial patterns across map sheets and years.

Abstract
Generalist robot policies and language-model agents provide complementary capabilities, but completing a task also requires adapting their use to the consequences of physical interaction. We study execution as a locus of adaptation: feedback guides the current action and supplies evidence for revising subsequent decisions under fixed model weights. We introduce RoboDoctor, a training-free framework that connects diagnosis, grounded action, and persistent experience across three revision targets. The harness allocates control between policy and agent; intervention determines when to assist while preserving useful policy behavior; and operation refines physical methods through observed effects. Evolve on Graph: Self-Evolving Knowledge Graph Reasoning without Question Annotations
Authors: Zongyuan Sun, Shuai Zhao, Ke Shu, Ziyue Zhu, Shangyang Wu, Yuanlin Lin, Tao Wang, Shiyue Li, Yingjun Du, Anh Tuan Luu, Haoran Luo
First: 2026-10-26T15:23:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T15:23:04+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Knowledge graphs (KGs) provide reliable external knowledge for multi-hop factual reasoning with large language models (LLMs), yet most KGs in real-world domains such as finance and industry lack question--answer (QA) annotations. Without such annotations, existing methods struggle to improve LLM graph reasoning: training-free agents cannot improve their exploration through interaction; reinforcement learning (RL) agents depend on QA data annotated by humans or synthesized by closed-source models; and training data is typically built offline, so its topology mix is fixed and cannot follow the agent's ability. We propose Evolve on Graph (EoG), a framework that lets an LLM self-evolve its graph reasoning without any QA annotations. Through Graph Self-Play, a single LLM alternates between a Proposer that poses questions and a Solver that answers them. To make questions verifiable, Symbolically Verifiable Topology Synthesis (SVTS) samples reasoning topologies on the KG and executes the corresponding first-order logic queries, yielding topology skeletons with correct and complete answer sets; guided by a skeleton, the Proposer explores the graph to pose a multi-hop question. To let task topologies follow the Solver, a Boundary-Aware Graph Curriculum rewards the Proposer by the Solver's score variance, keeping questions at the Solver's capability boundary so that complex topologies enter training as the Solver improves. On six benchmarks over Freebase, Wikidata, and an industrial KG, EoG consistently outperforms prompting-, retrieval-, and agent-based methods, showing that LLM graph reasoning can improve through self-play without QA annotations. Our code is available at https://github.com/Szy5/EoG

Abstract
Many everyday robot manipulation skills are affordance-dependent, with success determined by whether the robot contacts the functional object region required by the subsequent action. Current simulation data generators obtain contacts from generic grasp estimators or per-object manual contact annotations, but generic estimators rank stable grasps without task semantics and often select contacts that are misaligned with the downstream action, while manual contact annotations must be rewritten for each new object and task. To solve these challenges, we introduce AffordSim, a scalable data generator and benchmark that integrates open-vocabulary 3D affordance prediction into simulation-based trajectory generation. Given a natural-language task description, AffordSim synthesizes a task-relevant scene, emits affordance queries, grounds them on object surfaces, samples region-conditioned grasps, and selects executable candidates with motion planning. It further randomizes object pose, texture, lighting, image noise, and cross-viewpoint backgrounds for sim-to-real transfer. We instantiate AffordSim as a 50-task benchmark across diverse manipulation skills, five robot embodiments, and 500+ rigid and articulated objects. AffordSim achieves 93% of the trajectory collection success rate of manual contact annotations on affordance-critical tasks and 89% on hard composite tasks. Vision-language-action policies trained on AffordSim data transfer zero-shot to a real Franka FR3, reaching 24% average success.

Abstract
Uniform frame sampling can miss brief evidence in sparse-event videos when the VLM sees only K frames. We propose EARS (Evidence-Aware Retrieval via Sound and motion), a training-free, query-free front-end that ranks frames using optical-flow motion and audio energy, then applies temporal non-maximum suppression for diversity. EARS therefore targets low-budget videos whose salient evidence aligns with motion or audio; on dense narratives or stealthy events, uniform or semantic selection remains preferable, and cheap modality statistics cannot reliably route between them. CANS: Capability-Preserving Neuron Steering for Large Language Models
Authors: Yucheng Wu, Chengyi Xie, Chenpeng Wang, Liangming Pan
First: 2026-10-26T14:45:23+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T14:45:23+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Activation steering offers training-free and dynamic control of target behaviors in large language models, but existing methods can impair general capabilities and require domain-specific intervention designs. We introduce Capability-Preserving Neuron Steering (CANS), a unified framework that balances target-behavior control with capability preservation. To make target-behavior improvement and general-capability preservation tractable, we introduce two proxies: log-probabilities of target tokens in model generated responses, and output-distribution KL divergence on general-purpose inputs. Combining the local approximation of the two proxies yields a closed-form solution, enabling lightweight steering without iterative model training or additional inference-time computation. The resulting neuron-wise scaling coefficients achieve effective steering while limiting general-capability loss. A shared data-construction and intervention procedure supports adaptation across domains using sampled responses with target-behavior filtering and target-span annotations. Across safety, toxicity, and hallucination control on Qwen and Llama models, CANS achieves comparable or better steering while better preserving MMLU, BBH, and IFEval performance than SFT, CAA, and neuron-level baselines. On Qwen3-4B, CANS reduces HarmBench attack success from $15.50\%$ to $1.75\%$ with a $1.11$-point decrease in average capability. These results support CANS as a practical approach to behavioral control across domains.

WorldPager: Zero-Stall Tiered Memory for Long-Horizon World Models on a Single Consumer GPU
Authors: Bingnan Liu, Ruini Xue
First: 2026-10-26T14:44:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T14:44:33+00:00
Comments: 11 pages, 4 figures, 9 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Interactive world models keep the KV of past chunks as retrieval memory so that revisited places stay consistent. On a single consumer machine (one RTX 4090, 32 GiB of host memory, one NVMe SSD), long sessions with such memory hit two walls. The first is capacity: the KV of Matrix-Game 2.0 exhausts host memory after 45 seconds of video in bf16 and after 2.8 minutes in int4. The second is the model's positional horizon of 4.3 minutes. We show that this memory is capacity-bound rather than bandwidth-bound and that its access set is known before compute, because the camera pose is an input. An analytical model built on these observations predicts spill points to within one chunk and classifies zero versus non-zero stalls correctly on held-out configurations. Guided by it, WorldPager stores int4 KV in a GPU/DRAM/NVMe hierarchy instead of recomputing it, prefetches each chunk's memory from the extrapolated pose, never waits on a miss, and writes back in the background; to our knowledge, it is the first to place the KV memory of an interactive world model on an NVMe tier with zero-stall prefetching. With training-free position handling, it generates 37.5 minutes of video continuously with zero stalls and constant host memory, at about 9.5 frames per second. On a sealed test set, int4 storage shows no measurable quality loss; on long-gap revisits it lowers image quality by about one MUSIQ point.

Abstract
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has delivered impressive gains in mathematical and multimodal reasoning and has become a standard post-training paradigm for contemporary language and vision-language models. However, the RLVR recipe introduces a significant risk of capability regression, in which models forget foundational skills after prolonged training without employing regularization strategies. We empirically confirm this concern, observing that open-source reasoning models suffer performance degradation on core capabilities such as perception and faithfulness. While imposing regularization terms like KL divergence can help prevent deviation from the base model, these terms are computed on the current task and therefore do not guarantee preservation of broader knowledge. Meanwhile, commonly used experience replay across heterogeneous domains makes it nontrivial to decide how much training emphasis each objective should receive. To address this, we propose RECAP-a replay strategy with dynamic objective reweighting for general knowledge preservation. Our reweighting mechanism adapts online using short-horizon signals of convergence and instability, shifting the post-training focus away from saturated objectives and toward underperforming or volatile ones. Our method is end-to-end and readily applicable to existing RLVR pipelines without training additional models or heavy tuning. Extensive experiments on benchmarks using Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, which not only preserves general capabilities but also improves reasoning by enabling more flexible trade-offs among in-task rewards.

Abstract
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) map complex visual inputs to semantic spaces, but interpreting the cross-modal reasoning of VLMs currently relies on post-hoc explainers evaluated via unimodal perturbation metrics. We expose a limitation in this paradigm: because multimodal datasets contain language priors and modality biases, VLMs frequently exhibit cross-modal redundancy, allowing them to answer visual queries using text alone. By decoupling visual plausibility from cross-modal faithfulness, this work provides a rigorous evaluation framework required to safely audit VLM reasoning in high-stakes deployments. Abstract
Recent studies have introduced memory-augmented architectures that decouple knowledge storage from neural computation, enabling more direct access to stored representations. However, existing approaches often rely on learned memory embeddings or explicit memory and retrieval components, requiring additional training or increasing architectural complexity. To address these limitations, we propose N-gram Memory (NGM), a training-free, plug-and-play module that repurposes a backbone model's pretrained token embedding space as memory. Furthermore, NGM extends beyond text-only language models to vision-language models, with Qwen3-VL-2B achieving improvements across all five VLM benchmarks. Diffusion language models (DLMs) promise parallel text generation, but in practice face an unresolved speed-quality trade-off. Speculative decoding is the standard lossless acceleration for autoregressive models. However, it does not transfer directly to DLMs, which must choose both what to generate and where. We show that this order is the missing piece: the model's own predictions from the previous pass already give the right token at over $90\%$ of positions, but not which position comes next. Coupled-noise match, which shares Gumbel noise between drafter and target, keeps this verification exact at any temperature. This approach also inspired us to introduce SPOT-Sets for dynamic threshold decoding, where a pass commits several positions: SPOT-Sets drafts whole commit sets together with their tokens. Our comprehensive experiments show that SPOT cuts target passes by up to $6.8\times$ and reaches up to $3.4\times$ the throughput of standard decoding on SDAR-8B, matching or beating the calibrated and training-free drafting baselines in every task and budget at $T=0$ and $T=1$. In addition, we show that SPOT's learned drafter transfers across models, demonstrating successful transfer across six different DLMs; with a light calibration, the SPOT-Sets drafter transfers as well. Abstract
Post-training compression of transformer models commonly relies on truncated singular value decomposition (SVD) strategies leading to low-rank approximations of the original model weights.
